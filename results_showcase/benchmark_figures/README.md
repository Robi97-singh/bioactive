# Benchmark Comparison Figures

Publication figures for the backbone bioactivity benchmark, styled after the two most important
plots of Fredin Haslum et al. (*Nature Communications* 15:3470, 2024): the per-assay ROC-AUC
boxplot (their Fig 1c) and the model comparison with Friedman + Nemenyi statistical testing
(their Fig 2a). All results are on JUMP-CP source_11, 29 assays, 6-fold CV (every arm, including
LoRA, now uses the full 6-fold CV). Generated by `make_benchmark_figures.py`,
`make_adaptation_figure.py`, and `make_pr_calibration_pareto.py`.

## Findings

1. **Domain-matched pretraining wins.** Cell-DINO (pretrained on Cell Painting) significantly beats generic ImageNet DINOv2-Base (Nemenyi p=0.0044) and DINOv2-Large (p=0.0139) -- with a smaller model in both cases.
2. **Scale alone does not help, frozen.** DINOv2-Large (304M params) is statistically indistinguishable from DINOv2-Base (86M params) -- p=0.9974 -- despite a 3.5x increase in parameters, holding architecture and pretraining domain fixed. This isolates parameter count as not the active ingredient behind CLIP-L's earlier scale advantage; pretraining data volume/diversity is the more likely driver. This finding replicates at 448 resolution too: both DINOv2 variants gain a near-identical ~1 point from 224->448 (see Fig C and finding 4), reinforcing that scale changes neither raw performance nor resolution sensitivity for this backbone family.
3. **"Biomedical" is not domain-matched.** BiomedCLIP is statistically indistinguishable from generic ImageNet DINOv2-Base (p=0.9938) and DINOv2-Large (p=1.0000) -- nominal biomedical relevance does not help. It is, however, now significantly worse than Cell-DINO (p=0.0181), reinforcing that domain-matched pretraining specifically (not just "biomedical" branding) is what matters.
4. **Resolution is model-dependent.** It helps fine-tuned ResNet (+1.5 pts), and frozen DINOv2-Base (+0.94 pts) and DINOv2-Large (+0.98 pts) almost identically, but not frozen Cell-DINO (pretrained at 128px, ~0 change). A frozen backbone benefits from higher resolution only when it stays close to its pretraining resolution -- and this holds regardless of backbone scale.
5. **Cheap adaptation recovers most of the gap.** LoRA (2.4% of parameters trainable), now validated on the full 6-fold CV (per-fold: 0.6082, 0.6426, 0.6233, 0.6677, 0.6456, 0.6364; mean 0.6373 +/- 0.0203), reaches well above every frozen backbone, recovering **~68.5%** of the frozen->fine-tuned gap over DINOv2-Base -- substantially higher than earlier partial-fold estimates (~33% at 1 fold, ~55% at 3 folds), underscoring just how much a partial-fold estimate can mislead for a regime with wide fold-to-fold variance (LoRA's std, 0.0203, is roughly 3x every frozen arm's). With the full 6 folds in, LoRA sits much closer to full fine-tuning (within 0.0265) than to any frozen arm. Fig H visualizes this as a single performance-vs-parameter-cost plot.
6. **Fine-tuning remains the strongest option, though the margin over domain-matched frozen features -- and now over cheap adaptation -- is narrower than it first appeared.** ResNet significantly beats DINOv2-Base, DINOv2-Large, and BiomedCLIP (all p<0.001) in the frozen-arm Nemenyi test, but the gap to Cell-DINO specifically is no longer statistically significant at the 5-arm correction (p=0.0605, borderline), and LoRA -- while not part of that formal test -- lands within 0.0265 of ResNet's fine-tuned mean at a fraction of the training cost.
7. **No arm produces calibrated probabilities.** Pooling every fold and assay per arm (Fig G), predicted probabilities range roughly 0.2-0.75 but the observed positive rate never exceeds ~0.06 in any bin, for every one of the 6 arms. All models are substantially overconfident. This does not affect the ranking-based comparisons above (ROC-AUC and the Friedman/Nemenyi tests depend only on score ordering), but raw model outputs should not be read as true activity probabilities -- e.g. for setting a fixed decision threshold in downstream compound triage, a calibration step (such as Platt scaling or isotonic regression) would be needed first.
8. **Absolute precision is low across all arms, consistent with class imbalance.** Average Precision ranges 0.041-0.051 (Fig F), only modestly above the random baseline (AP=0.030), reflecting that active compounds are rare in most assays. The relative ranking under this class-imbalance-robust metric matches the ROC-AUC-based findings above exactly (ResNet50 > LoRA > Cell-DINO > DINOv2-Base ~ DINOv2-Large > BiomedCLIP), reinforcing that the main comparisons are not an artifact of ROC-AUC's known insensitivity to imbalance.

## Design decisions

**1. Figure B is 224-only and frozen + ResNet, by design.** The rigorous statistical comparison
uses a single matched resolution (224) so the Friedman/Nemenyi test isolates the backbone
question from resolution. Mixing 224/448 variants would confound the two.

**2. LoRA is shown in Figure D, not Figure B.** LoRA is a structurally different training regime
(partial-parameter adaptation via low-rank adapters, trained with AdamW at its own learning
rate) from both the frozen linear-probe arms (SGD, one shared recipe) and full fine-tuning
(ResNet50, its own recipe). Mixing a differently-trained regime into the Friedman/Nemenyi test
built for comparing frozen backbones under one fixed recipe would not be a like-for-like
comparison, regardless of fold count. LoRA now has the same full 6-fold CV as every other arm
(this was completed after an initial partial-fold estimate; see finding 5), but it remains a
separate regime, shown in the adaptation-spectrum figure (Fig D) rather than folded into Fig B's
statistical test.

**3. 448 exists only for the DINO-family backbones.** BiomedCLIP and CLIP hard-downsample input
to 224 internally and use fixed 224px positional embeddings, so a genuine 448 arm is not
possible without positional-embedding surgery and off-distribution extrapolation of a CLIP
image-text projection. They are evaluated at 224 only and excluded from Figure C. DINOv2-Large's
448 arm is complete and included in Figure C (see finding 2 and 4).

**4. The resolution effect is a finding, not an omission** -- see finding 4.

**5. Per-sample predictions are now saved for every arm; PR and calibration curves (Figs F, G)
were built instead of literal per-assay ROC curves.** Originally, the frozen-probe pipeline
(`train_head.py`) saved only per-assay AUC scalars, not per-sample predictions, so any ROC-style
curve would have needed a re-inference pass. This has since been resolved: `train_head.py` now
also saves raw per-sample sigmoid probabilities and labels (`test_preds.csv` / `test_labels.csv`,
matching the format the fine-tuned/LoRA pipeline already used), via a cheap re-run against the
already-cached embeddings (no backbone re-computation, no retraining). Given this data, pooled
Precision-Recall curves (Fig F) and calibration curves (Fig G) were prioritized over literal
per-assay ROC curves, since PR is the more informative choice under the class imbalance these
assays show (finding 8), and calibration surfaces a real, otherwise-invisible limitation (finding
7). Literal ROC-curve overlays are straightforward to add from the same saved files if wanted.

## Reproducing

```bash
# in a pod with pandas / matplotlib / scipy / scikit-posthocs / scikit-learn:
python make_benchmark_figures.py         # figs A, B, C, E + Nemenyi + summary
python make_adaptation_figure.py         # fig D (adaptation spectrum); auto-discovers whatever
                                          # LoRA fold results exist under
                                          # results/bioact_lora_vit_s_r224_fold*/plots/per_assay_auc.csv
python make_pr_calibration_pareto.py     # figs F, G, H; reads test_preds.csv / test_labels.csv
                                          # per fold per arm (frozen arms: .../plots/; LoRA and
                                          # ResNet: directly under the fold directory)
# read /shared/ssd/logs/b-r-singh1/results/cv/{arm}/cv_per_assay.csv
# write figures to results/figures/
```
